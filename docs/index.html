Epochs

From an atlas of sciences to a habit inside the model.

One goal across every stage: a model that checks before it claims and says not enough data when the deciding number is missing.

Epoch 1 · 2005 to 2023 · done

Twenty years of filtering.

The sciences filtered and collected into one atlas of sciences.

Every later epoch stands on it.

Epoch 2 · 2024 to 2026 · done

Discipline outside the model.

The atlas turned into 16 rules wrapped around the model at answer time, with no retraining. On our ONTO-Bench (268 questions, 110 of them unanswerable), recall of unanswerable questions rose from under 0.10 to 0.96, with many false alarms (U-F1 0.58). It was scored by our earlier regex engine, since retired; the multipliers built on it are withdrawn. A regulator dashboard was built on it and offered in one government tender.

It proved the behaviour can be produced. But a layer can be removed, and a copy of the model does not inherit it.

Epoch 3 · 2026 · now

Discipline in the weights.

The same habit trained into the weights of the fully open OLMo-2 7B, with no tool and no prompt around it. On three fresh closed sets of 60 real claims, keyed before each run: 55, 48 and 50 right, against 28, 33 and 32 for the untrained model. On topics it was never trained on, the habit did not carry over: a preregistered transfer test failed.

Every run is preregistered and its key fingerprinted: frozen keys. Try it: the live model.

Epoch 4 · next

Personality through discipline.

Checking before claiming as a stable trait, measured by three tests. Without being asked to be careful: yes, in its own domain. Across topics: not yet, the transfer test failed at 7B. Under pressure: not measured yet. The trait counts only when all three pass on closed sets.

Horizon

A base layer for general AI.

The direction, not a result: general-purpose and embodied AI that inherits the habit of checking before it claims. Each step toward it is judged the same way, on closed sets with keys frozen before the run.

Measured

Where does the possible end?

A library of physical limits: Betz, Carnot, the first law, luminous efficacy, Shockley and Queisser.

Measured

Does the model know the limit?

A frontier model catches 44 of 47 impossible claims, with 0 false alarms on 36 possible ones.

Measured

Does it say not enough data when it should?

Only in 25 of 45 cases. It fills in the missing number and answers anyway.

Measured

Is the knowledge inside the model?

Yes. A probe reads it at 0.81 and 0.78 in two open 7B models. Their words sit at 0.51 and 0.49.

Measured

Can training connect knowing and saying?

Yes. A fine-tune of 0.04% of the weights lifts the spoken answer from 0.52 to 0.80. Trained into OLMo-2 7B: 55, 48 and 50 of 60 on three fresh closed sets, against 28, 33 and 32 untrained.

Partly

Does it hold without help, under pressure, on new claims?

In its own domain, yes, with no tool and no prompt. On unseen topics, no: a preregistered transfer test failed (16 of 21 missing numbers found, but 10 of 21 false alarms). Under pressure: not measured yet.

What we got wrong

Results we withdrew.

42 to 47 of 47

We reported that our library took a frontier model from 42 to 47. A clean rerun gave 25 to 27 of 29, p = 0.625.

Reflex and mirror

Earlier fine-tunes taught refusing everywhere or agreeing with lies. An outside audit traced it to one-sided labels and a reward where not enough data was the cheapest answer.

Reading the signal

We first read the fine-tune as using the probe signal. An ablation did not support it, so we changed the claim.
